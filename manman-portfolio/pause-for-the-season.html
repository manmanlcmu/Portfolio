Pause for the Season

One minute a day to feel the season you are in.

About the project

01

Why I made this

I have always lived in cities with four clear seasons: Beijing, then Tokyo, now Pittsburgh. And the two cultures I call home, Chinese and Japanese, both celebrate every turn of the year.

Still, on busy days, the seasons slip past me. It is often my family who brings me back. Look at the clouds today. The leaves have turned yellow. The air is getting dry. Small hints from the season, asking me to feel the day a little differently.

This app is that gentle reminder, for anyone. Sixty seconds a day, a work of art and a few quiet words to wake up the senses, so we can live each precious day with all five of them.

02

How it works

The year is divided into 72 micro-seasons of about five days each. Every day opens with one card: the weather, the current micro-season and what it means, and a work of art chosen for it. Then three slow breaths with a lantern glow, and a quiet close.

03

Designed to be small

The whole ritual fits in about a minute. No feed, no scores, nothing to catch up on. The goal is not more time on a screen, but a small daily habit of noticing the world around you.

Inspiration

Where the idea came from

七十二候

The 72 micro-seasons

An ancient calendar from China, later adapted in Japan, that names each five-day stretch of the year after a small change in nature: “Chrysanthemums bloom yellow,” “Frogs start singing.” I grew up with it in Beijing and lived with it in Tokyo.

Art

Art, in many forms

Each micro-season is paired with a work of art: a painting, a woodblock print, a photograph, a sculpture or an installation. Art asks us to slow down and look closely, the same way the seasons do. Many pieces come from museum open-access collections, like Maxfield Parrish’s The Lantern Bearers (1908).

Senses

Running, hiking and cooking

I feel the seasons most when I run or hike. My mind lets go of everything else, and I simply take in where I am: the air on my skin, each breath, the smell of the trail, the changing light. I find the same feeling when I cook with seasonal ingredients. That might just be my next side project.

Thank you for noticing.
